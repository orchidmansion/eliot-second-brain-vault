Our A/B testing shows that this assumption isn’t reliable. SEO and GEO aren’t the same thing. In fact, the same on-page change can elevate LLM-driven discovery while harming your organic performance on Google or vice versa. A win in one channel does not necessarily equal a win in the other. We have been applying our A/B testing approach to the world of AI and GEO. The testing method stays the same. The pages you’re testing stay the same. You're no longer testing against a single search result, but against an entire AI-driven research journey.

- **Brand USP modules increased  LLM traffic by +18% for Omio **

### **The hypothesis **

We tested whether adding brand USPs to its site would have a positive impact on LLM traffic because LLMs tend to surface content that clearly explains what makes a brand different. USPs quickly communicate why a user should choose one provider over another. 

### **The outcome **

The test showed a clear uplift in LLM sessions, indicating that adding the brand USPs was consistent with improved visibility in LLM-driven journeys. The impact on Google organic sessions was inconclusive. 

### **The change **

We added clear, well-structured brand USPs to make it easier for LLMs to understand and summarise the page’s value. Our hypothesis was that this would increase the likelihood of these pages being cited or linked to in LLM-driven responses, leading to an uplift in LLM sessions on variant pages compared to control pages. 

### **The decision **

Given the clear benefit observed in LLM traffic and no evidence of harm to Google's organic performance, the result supported rolling out the USPs module across relevant pages. This is a good example of a GEO-first change that can add incremental value without harming SEO.

- **Testing prevented Omio from shipping a change that could hurt its biggest organic traffic channel **

### **The hypothesis **

LLMs may favor structured, summary-style content when interpreting and referencing pages. Our hypothesis was that adding ‘key takeaways’ would positively impact LLM traffic and make the page easier for LLMs to parse and summarize, but we would have to monitor any secondary impact on Google organic sessions. 

### **The change **

We added bulleted, quantitative and comparative on-page data above an existing summary table in the HTML. This improves clarity as well as providing data at a glance rather than at a granular level. 

### **LLM sessions Google organic**

### **The outcome **

 While the change performed positively for  LLM-driven traffic, it could hurt Google organic sessions by -6.5%. The test delivered mixed results across traffic sources, which could  be due to content overlaps, reduced prominence  of crawlable data or other important content,  or increased AI overview exposure leading  to fewer clicks. 

### **The decision **

 Because the primary goal was to improve how the page gets interpreted and summarised by LLMs, Omio considered the test positive. However, Google organic traffic is a critical channel for Omio, so the decision  was made not to roll out this change because of the negative impact. Instead, we have developed iterations of this test, exploring further iterations, to see whether a more balanced result can be achieved. This shows why testing is essential: a GEO win can still be an SEO risk.
