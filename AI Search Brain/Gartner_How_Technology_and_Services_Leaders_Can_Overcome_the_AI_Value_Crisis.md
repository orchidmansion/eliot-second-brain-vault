## 1. From “AI Features” to Outcome‑Based Search/SEO Programs

## The shift from digital → intelligence supercycle means:

## Stop selling/positioning “AI‑enhanced SEO” as:

## “We use LLMs for content at scale”

## “We automate meta tags / clustering / internal linking”

## Start framing AI initiatives as:

## Revenue and margin stories, not tooling:

## “+X% incremental non‑brand revenue from AI‑driven topic coverage”

## “Y% lower cost per content unit *with* maintained or improved organic revenue”

## “Reduced payback period on content investments by Z%”

## Actionable angle:Recast your AI+SEO roadmap under an outcome lens:

## For each AI capability (content generation, log analysis, internal linking, entity modelling), define:

## Target business outcome metric (revenue, margin, lead quality, market entry speed)

## Target timeframe (e.g., 2–3 quarters)

## How you will show CFO‑relevant proof, not “time saved”

## 2. Defend / Extend / Upend for SEO & Search

## Use Gartner’s framework to structure your portfolio:

## Defend (most current orgs)

## Use AI to:

## Scale content ops, briefs, QA

## Automate technical monitoring and issue triage

## Improve reporting and insight generation

## KPIs: time saved, coverage, incident reduction

## This keeps you in the game, but doesn’t solve the AI value crisis alone.

## Extend

## Tie AI directly to growth and margin:

## AI‑driven demand sensing → enter new topic clusters/markets earlier

## AI‑powered pricing/search elasticity insights from query + behavior data

## AI‑based creative testing for SERP CTR uplift

## KPIs: incremental revenue, margin uplift, CAC/ROAS improvements

## Upend

## High‑risk, high‑reward bets where search + AI redefine the model:

## Moving from “SEO as acquisition” to SEO as a continuous intent intelligence system feeding product, pricing, and CX.

## Vertical or brand‑owned assistants/agents that sit between user and search engine (e.g., your own “domain‑specific agent” for car buying, B2B procurement, etc.).

## KPIs: new revenue streams, new products, new markets, “return on the future.”

## Actionable angle:Build a portfolio view of your search/SEO initiatives:

## Tag each initiative as Defend / Extend / Upend

## Ensure your Upend allocation is > 0 and explicitly funded (e.g., 10–20% of AI/search budget)

## 3. Domain‑Specific Models & Vertical Search Advantage

## Public LLMs struggle with:

## Complex, regulatory, or high‑stakes queries

## Deep domain nuance (e.g., automotive configuration, complex B2B procurement)

## Domain‑specific models:

## Encode industry vocabulary, constraints, guarantees

## Can be paired with search logs, analytics, product data to create:

## Vertical copilots (e.g., “auto buyer advisor”, “enterprise SaaS selector”)

## Internal SEO intelligence agents (e.g., “international SEO strategist” that knows your SKUs, locales, and constraints)

## Actionable angle:For your brands/sectors:

## Identify 1–2 high‑value journeys where generic search + generic LLMs underperform (e.g., complex high‑consideration purchases).

## Explore a domain‑tuned model or agent that:

## Uses your content, product catalog, and performance data

## Surfaces actions (price bands, bundles, content gaps) instead of just answers

## Channel/locale/device nuances

## Past brand interactions

## Customize:

## Map what “memory” you have today (logs, CRM, CDP, product, content metadata).

## Prioritize 1–2 high‑value use cases for context‑aware agents:

## For users (personalized site search / buying guides)

## For internal teams (agent that remembers past tests, local markets, constraints when making SEO/SEM recommendations)

## Core Theme: The AI Value Crisis

The webinar argues we’re in an “AI value crisis”:

Massive hyperscaler and enterprise AI spend vs. relatively weak, tangible business outcomes.

Public marketing claims “value at scale,” but in private, vendors still ask how to actually *deliver* that value.

Result: a widening gap between AI hype and realized ROI.

Key data points:

91% of service/support leaders feel strong pressure from executives to “do something with AI.”

88% of CIOs report mainly intangible value (e.g., time saved).

Only 36% of CFOs have confidence that AI will deliver tangible benefits.Errol calls this the “cocktail of misery.”

## From Digital Supercycle → Intelligence Supercycle

We’re moving from the digital supercycle (SaaS, features, speed, cost reduction) to the intelligence supercycle, which is about:

Business value and outcomes,

Monetization of results (for both buyers and vendors),

Outcome‑driven solutions instead of product/feature selling.

The old SaaS playbook (POCs, per‑seat pricing, frictionless adoption) doesn’t fit AI, where success is closer to bespoke tailoring:

Not “how many models/tokens?” but “did this change the business outcome?”

New economic models are emerging, e.g., vendors taking a cut of incremental profit instead of classic IT project budgets.

## Tokenomics & Model Strategy

To avoid “driving a Ferrari to buy a loaf of bread”:

You must right‑size models: don’t use ultra‑expensive, general LLMs for simple tasks.

Large providers are already using routers to send prompts to the cheapest effective model.

Providers have a responsibility to guide customers toward economically appropriate models and model diversity aligned to task complexity.

## Agentic AI, Context Layer & Gartner Agentic Compass

Errol frames an agentic AI spectrum:

Y‑axis: enterprise value / pricing power.

X‑axis: complexity and variability of the problem.

The current competitive frontier:

Memory: reusing prior context instead of recomputing from scratch.

Reasoning: understanding questions better, using fewer tokens and giving more accurate answers.

This “context layer” is emerging as the new moat.

Gartner’s Agentic Compass (based on 200+ agentic solutions and 1000+ deployments) finds highest ROI correlates with:

Workflow complexity (working across complex flows),

Cross‑domain operation,

Personalization (tailored to stakeholders),

Human collaboration (orchestrating teams and functions).

Through-line: personalization → trust → more engagement → scaled adoption.

## Defend, Extend, Upend: AI Strategy Model

Gartner categorizes enterprise AI strategies as:

Defend (where ~88% of CIOs are stuck)

Fear‑driven: “We must use AI so we don’t fall behind.”

Focused on internal productivity, time savings, RoE.

Often reactive, buying into hype.

Extend

Use AI to improve top line (revenue) and bottom line (margin).

Typical use cases: customer service, sales enablement, marketing uplift, SDLC automation.

Upend

Faith‑based, high‑risk, high‑reward strategy.

Reimagines the future of the business (e.g., 2035 customers, value props, operating model).

Requires dedicated budget and time to place bets beyond incremental ROI.

Methods like design thinking, innovation labs, “walking the halls,” asset‑based consulting are expected to see a renaissance.

Leaders must adopt a venture mindset:

Define a risk index (high‑risk/high‑reward vs. mainstream vs. low‑risk/low‑reward customers) and serve each bucket differently.

Continuously revisit this as the AI race and customer base evolve.

## Emerging Tech Disruptors & AI Vendor Race

Errol highlights several disruptive trends reshaping the AI landscape:

Domain‑specific models

By 2030, Gartner expects 90% of GenAI‑enabled solutions to rely on domain‑specific models.

“One‑size‑fits‑all” public LLMs don’t scale across complex enterprise use cases – the value paradox.

Agentic AI & disaggregated applications

AI increasingly unbundles SaaS, embedding intelligence into workflows rather than monolithic apps (“SaaS apocalypse” notion).

Native AI races emerge in domains where AI creates entirely new categories.

Physical AI

Especially in asset‑intensive industries and defense, where AI controls or augments physical systems.

Hyper synthetic data

Required to simulate extreme or rare conditions.

Example: Coca‑Cola’s synthetic population to optimize vending machine placement.

## Application Cortex & Intelligent Simulation

For AI at scale, enterprises need an “application cortex”:

A top‑down orchestration layer that sits over domains and systems, coordinating AI decisions.

It meets bottom‑up, domain‑specific processes in the middle.

This requires:

Governance and orchestration of AI “real estate” (which models/agents run where and why).

Intelligent simulation to test scenarios before deployment (e.g., cyber simulations, economic impact simulations).

## Cyber‑Kinetic Security & Digital Trust

As digital systems deeply integrate with physical operations:

Cyber‑kinetic security becomes a board‑level fiduciary duty.

Metrics shift toward dynamic attack surface reduction and AI‑driven safety architectures.

At the same time, digital trust is eroding:

Disinformation and synthetic media force enterprises to invest heavily (>$25B cited) in content authentication.

Builders need consolidated digital trust/authenticity platforms to stay ahead of regulation and maintain credibility.

## Customer Story & Takeaway

A large global services provider:

Thought its most advanced AI customers and internal “rock stars” were edge cases.

Realized those outliers actually represent the new normal in the intelligence supercycle.

Is now reorganizing around scaling those capabilities instead of treating them as exceptions.

Overall takeaway:To overcome the AI value crisis, tech and services leaders must:

Move from product/feature selling → outcome/value selling.

Treat AI as bespoke, outcome‑oriented tailoring, not generic SaaS.

Build token‑savvy, domain‑specific, agentic, and context‑rich solutions.

Adopt a venture portfolio approach to risk across defend/extend/upend.

Invest early in application cortex, simulation, cyber‑kinetic security, and digital trust as structural capabilities.

How Technology and Services Leaders Can Overcome the AI Value Crisis

Understand how the AI Value Crisis emerged — and what it means for your strategy and customers

Win stakeholder alignment by shifting leadership mindsets from fear‑based delay to future‑ready investment

Anticipate and accelerate success using emerging disruptive technologies shaping the Intelligence Supercycle

Monetize AI capabilities by aligning products and services to tangible, defensible business outcomes

The AI industry is hurtling toward a major correction. Investments are at an all‑time high, yet enterprise buyers have become increasingly skeptical — paralyzed by commoditized capabilities, unclear differentiation and stalled proof‑of‑concepts. The result is an escalating AI value crisis, underscored by the projection that by 2030, the gap between Hyperscaler AI spend and end‑user revenue will exceed $300 billion.

Technology and services leaders now face intensifying pressure as buyers push back on pricing, question ROI and struggle to scale AI into profitable returns. This webinar will explore how technology and services leaders can navigate this volatile transition into the Intelligence Supercycle. We’ll discuss how to realign solutions around measurable business outcomes, overcome behavioral resistance through role‑tailored literacy, shift customers from defensive hesitation to bold market‑making initiatives, and successfully monetize AI capabilities in a crowded, skeptical market.
