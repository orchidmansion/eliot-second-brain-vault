## **Five ideas from the session
**

**1. Prompts are the inverse of keywords. The tail is the whole game.
**

Real user data shows searches average three and a half words - mostly head terms, almost no tail left. Prompts are the opposite: long, complex, attribute-loaded, almost all tail. “Men’s soap, monthly subscription, different scents, quality” is a normal prompt and a search nobody would ever type. This is why AI search is additive to SEO, and why search data alone can’t tell you what to optimize for.

**2. The pages winning AI answers are pages SEOs never optimized.
**

Granola wins the meeting-notes prompt partly from its careers page. Adobe wins Premiere Pro questions from three help center pages. Dr. Squatch wins from category, product, and subscribe pages simultaneously. Traditional SEO landing pages were mostly absent from every example Ethan pulled. The new surface area: help centers, integrations pages, job posts, press releases, and - from the discussion - even compliance PDFs linked in footers.

**3. Cannibalization is dead. Consensus is the strategy.
**

An SEO would say four pages competing for one keyword is a problem. In AI search, multiple pages and page types from your domain answering the same question is exactly what gets you picked - the model wants corroboration. Same logic off-site: the goal is many domains and many pages agreeing about you, which is why affiliates, review sites, and media matter as much as Reddit.

**4. Plant landmines. Say what you fulfill.
**

Granola’s “we don’t have an awkward bot joining your calls” line - buried in a job posting - became ChatGPT’s stated reason for recommending them. If you know the attributes buyers put in prompts (aluminum-free, integrates with X, easy to cancel), make sure they’re explicitly on your pages. The model can’t know you fulfill an intent you never acknowledge. Most brands cover the head questions and miss the tail of attributes entirely.

**5. On Reddit: never promote, always answer.
**

Ethan got a lifetime ban for introducing himself and his agency in an SEO thread. What works instead: answer questions when people ask about you (Webflow’s model), run authentic AMAs with real experts (the JUST Food for Dogs vet AMA that pushed down negative threads), be a genuine community member, and respond to criticism dispassionately - his own hate thread ended with the moderator inviting him to do an AMA. You can’t remove negative content. You push it down by creating positive.

## **Tools and resources mentioned
**

**The keyword-to-prompt workflow**: start with a target keyword, pull the ranking page’s full keyword set, have an LLM cluster them into themes, then generate prompt versions of each theme. Ethan demoed this live with “red lipstick.”

**Gemini for Reddit mining**: sort target subreddit questions by upvotes and comments as a popularity proxy. Ethan uses Gemini because it has the largest corpus of indexed Reddit threads.

**Bing Webmaster Tools grounding queries**: real first-party data on the queries Copilot runs for your topics. One of the few actual data sources in a space full of proxies. (Third guest in a row to point at this report.)

**Ask the model why**: after any answer, ask “why did you recommend these?” It often genuinely explains its rationale - which attributes it weighted, which pages fed the answer.

**Graphite’s “how did you hear about us” flow**: channel, then which assistant, then which prompt. Launched a week before the session. The most precise prompt-level attribution Ethan has found.

**The n8n case study**: 9% of conversions self-reported from AI versus 0.9% by last touch. The same 10x gap Kevin Indig reported. Also worth noting the Similarweb finding Ethan cited: only around 7% of AI answers carry citations at all, meaning most answers come from the core model, not RAG.

## **Three questions for the community
**

Ethan’s examples all won from non-traditional pages: help centers, integrations, careers, press releases. Which of these surfaces does your brand (or client) currently treat as an afterthought, and what would it take to bring it into scope?

What’s the “landmine” your product could plant - the differentiator competitors can’t claim that you’re not currently stating anywhere the model can read it?

Run the attribute test: pick your highest-value prompt and list every constraint a real buyer would include. How many of those attributes are explicitly stated on your pages today?
